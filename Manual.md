### CHAPTER 1.

### GENERAL INFORMATION ABOUT THE GAME CLASH.

#### Introduction:

An ancient powerful kingdom, extending from one sea to the other, fell into ruins, destroyed from inside by stupidity by lazy and bad sovereigns. Surrounded by splendour and flatterers, convinced that their empire would last forever. they did not feel that they were losing the land. Ambitious dukes and governors, who governed their properties, cut off bigger and bigger parts for themselves, and finally one of them, the most audacious or stupid - it’s hard to say - declared himself the sovereign of his “kingdom”. The king, who currently ruled, was too old and too weak to restore order in the kingdom. Soon, after his death (the cause of which was said not to be a natural one) his five sons started a war for the heritage, which resulted in wars splitting the kingdom into several parts. This was, however, only the beginning of the end....

... For several decades, desperate and equally senseless war has been waged, between small duchies, dukes - plunderers, various headmen and successive candidates for one or the other provinces’ sovereign. Nobody remembers the former times of prosperity. Bubonic plague epidemics decimate the last few cities and depopulate villages. Desperate peasants gather into large bands, plundering and murdering everyone whom they meet, and also waging their own wars, but not for the power over one or other territory, but for grain, powder, or an emaciated cow, with milk from which one could feed starving children. Dead bodies lie unburried, towns fall into ruin, the culture decays, trade does not exist any more, money loses its value, a loaf of bread costs a piece of gold, for which in former times one could have lived a whole month, every night flames of fires blaze into the sky, and the cry of people being murdered do not bother anyone. For those who cannot stand that sort of life, for those, who have no other choice (than to die killed by their enemies), for those attracted by adventure and the unknown, the only hope is the land of Karkhan, discovered several years ago, which - according to wayfarers reports - is inhabited by dozens of small clans of barbarians wearing skin, praying to pagan gods. Even though the wayfarers have also reported about dragons, bloody sacrifices and druids using black magic, nobody believes in them. Many daredevils go into the unknown, towards the coast of karkhan, expecting easy booty. The barbarians should not be any hindrance, and the fecund grounds tilled by the former owners, would ensure a better harvest than the fields of the Old Country, destroyed by fire and sword. After all, it is better to become king of barbarians than to be nobody...

However, the daredevils have miscalculated. “The Barbarians” turn out to be a pretty well organised nation, which - although divided into permanently fighting clans - are able to unite quickly against the enemy, who try to impose new faith and customs. Dragons really exist, as well as gigantic grubs, samaras, demons, Pegasus, sorcerers... And what is worse, many of the immigrants who arrive at the New World together with their retinues, usurp the right for the title “Emperor of Karkhan”. Not only do they try to force out of Karkhan it’s native inhabitants, but they also fight each other with the same fury. The war, which they tried to escape by going overseas, has followed them, and each step of the war is marked with new corpses and smouldering ruins...

You are one of the daredevils, who has gone to conquer Karkhan (or a headman of one of the clans, which oppose the invaders). You are facing a great challenge - you have to defeat all your rivals and unify Karkhan under your rule. You have no choice - win or die!

#### Types of games:

Strategic game with elements of economy; 1-5 players, each player may be controlled by a person or by computer (there are several levels of skills of a player and two creeds). Individual independent mission play is also possible, played on seperate maps, as well as a “campaign” as an option, consisting of a cycle of several missions related to each other. There is a separate campaign for a player who leads Christians and for Heretics.

#### The purpose of the game:

The purpose of the game is to gain control over the World, by fulfilling the requirements of a victory. This means (for missions) total elimination of the presence of enemies on the map. The purposes of the victory for the course of campaign are specified in the introduction to each of them (shown when a campaign is chosen).

#### The regulations:

The game depends on proceeding successive series of rounds until one of the players fulfils the requirements of a victory. By “series of rounds” we mean executed by each player one round (in a fixed order). Each detachment you own, has a certain amount of Action Points (denoted AP in the sequel). The number of AP’s depends on many factors (tiredness, type of detachment, etc.) AP’s are spent on moving detachements along the map, as well as on other actions (attack, building work, etc.) AP’s are renewed after each round (which does not mean that their amount will always be the same). The cost of a movement by a square, on the main map, depends on the type of terrain. The lowest cost is of movements along the roads, the highest is of movements along the swamp’s rims and small mountains. Some types of terrain are not accessible at all for the detachements which move along the land’s surface (swamps, mountains). Flying detachments can move all over the map (at the same AP cost independently of the terrain).

#### Remarks:

The game starts in the early Middle Ages. The action is placed within a world of fantasy, where magic, dragons and demons are ordinary and common. During the game a player may spend a part of his/her resources on development of new detachments, on greater power or speed, one may also take over the technology from a conquered castle, or achieve a higher level of development by oneself. This means that during the play you gain many types of detachments, not necessarily the same and at the same rate as other players.

The most frequently used abbreviations:

- LMB - Left Mouse Button
- AP - Action Points
- RMB - Right Mouse Button

### CHAPTER 3.

### CONTROLLING

#### General remarks:

The game Clash is controlled by use of a mouse, both by clicking icons and by opening “menu” windows. Commands are given with LMB, and clicking RMB on an object results in showing an information window (information about a detachement or a building, information about the function of an icon). Keeping the cursor (in any mode) on a spot for a few seconds, will produce information: type of terrain and number of AP which must be used to conquer it by non-flying detachements. One spot may be occupied only by one unit at a time. One cannot pass through ones own units, while moving a selected detachement. Entering a spot occupied by an other player’s detachment or building means starting a war (or a siege).

#### 3.1. Main menu:

**This menu allows players to:**

- Load the current situation of the game.
- Set parameters related to screen scrolling speed, screen brightness, person’s movement animation, turning the sound of effects on/off, turning on/off animation of persons and occasional scenes.
- Choose a campaign or separate missions. In the “campaign” mode one also has to choose a religion, which one will represent. This is done by clicking one of the pictures, which will appear on the screen when choosing this mode. In the “mission” mode the number and type of players participating in a game is set there (human or computer players) and also the intelligence level of the computer players (individually for each of them) and the religion of a particular (human) player. This is done by clicking (a required number of times) the icons which symbolise the player. Also, the names of players and the choice of the game’s place are set there.
- Get information about game’s authors.
- Exit the game.

#### 3.2. Main map’s modes:

At the top, on the frame, there are windows with several useful game options. To make use of them, place the cursor on the top edge of the screen, to activate a menu. Then choose an option and open it with LMB. To choose a sub-option, one has to move the cursor onto the sub-option (keeping the LMB pressed) and then release the mouse’s button.

##### 1. System

- mission: states mission objective
- surrender: a player withdraws from the game without stopping it (if more then 1 human player plays)
- save game: writes down game’s current situation
- load game: loads a previous saved game
- options: see point 2 below
- exit: return to initial menu

If more than one player participates in the game, the Clash game allows a withdrawal of a player, without stopping the game. In such case all the buildings of the withdrawing player are blown up (disappear from the map, together with all his/her detachments, gold, peasants). All prisoners in those castles die, and all of the player’s men kept imprisoned, stay alive. All of his/her special squads and persons disappear, and the final statistics do not include the player’s results for comparison of achievements.

##### 2. Options

- sfx - turns on/off sound effects
- music - turns music on/off
- animation (movement and cut-scenes) on/off
- screen scrolling speed
- mouse speed
- screen brightness

##### 3. Map

This option sets the map setup mode, so that one could see either all the objects or only selected ones (only castles, for example).

##### 4. End of a round (end turn)

Ends our round of movements.

These are:

1. The icon switch between buildings / detachments modes. If the icon shows a globe, you are in “buildings” mode. If placed on any of your buildings, the cursor would change into a castle. ##### Curing (completing losses)

To cure detachment(s) one has to mark them, by clicking LMB on them, and then clicking the icon “curing”. The curing lasts approximately three rounds, independently of the losses. After completing the curing the detachment has life energy of 100%.

##### Training (increasing fighting skills)

To train a detachment we follow the same instructions as to cure it. The difference is that the training lasts 5 rounds. A detachment activates automatically after the training is completed. Each training adds one sword more into the status window (see Attributes / Status in Unit’s Characteristics). Trained detachment has a special symbol on its icon during the whole duration of the training.

#### Transfer of gold

This option allows transfer of gold from one castle to another. Gold is transported in litters, which can carry at most 100 pieces of gold (and, maybe, a taxman, but this is not so important). Convoying the litters can be done by joining other detachments to them (a litter is treated the same way as a detachment, but does not participate in a battle). One can also overtake enemies litters. It suffices to attack a litter that belongs to another player. If it is protected by other detachments - a battle will begin. After winning the battle (or if there was no convoy escorting the litter) the litter automatically changes “the club”, transferring under our command. (Usually it joins our detachment /army).

#### How to perform the transfer of gold?

Enter a village (inside it). Click the indicator No. 7. There the amount of gold to be sent is set, as well as the destination: outside the castle or to another castle (window No. 6). Click the icon “take outside” (No. 10). The transfer is performed automatically, i.e. litters make their way to the selected destination on their own. However, in case an enemy’s detachment occurs on their way, the route is cancelled and the player must take control over the litters.

#### Transfer of peasants

The rules are the same as for transfer of gold, although peasants go on foot, not in litters (in groups of at most 1000 men). The wandering peasants carry bundles (levy in mass have weapons). By attacking such peasants (provided they are not protected) you can take over the group. In a battle (with the guarding detachments) such peasants do not take part, and after a victory they go under your command automatically.

#### Plague

Apparently, this is not a fatal incident, because the plague possibility depends on the number of peasants inside a castle. A plague stays only in a selected castle and lasts about 5 rounds. During it the population of peasants is significantly reduced, even by ten percent. A hospital inside a castle decreases the chance of a plague, and after its outbreak reduces the losses. The presence of a plague in a castle is indicated by a symbol of a death’s-head in castle’s information (on the main map as well as inside the castle). This symbol can be seen only by the castle’s owner. An appropriate announcement will also appear. At the time of the plague’s outbreak it is too late for any action against it...

*Tips: if you plant on your enemy a detachment of peasants from a currently plague-riden castle, you will cause the plague to infect his/ her castle, when the peasants enter it, independently of the number of peasants located inside (unless the detachment is cured in the meantime, which can also happen). Infected peasants have a death’s-head symbol in their information (this can be seen only by their current owner). Obviously, their population decreases until the disease is over.*

#### 3.5. Controlling the detachments

##### Merging units:

Mark a unit and move it next to the unit you wish to merge it to. Choose the merging icon from the menu at the bottom of the screen. Click the cursor on the neighbouring detachment (the cursor should then change into the icon’s miniature). The marked unit will automatically merge into the indicated unit, creating an army. This command costs the merging unit 5 AP’s.

How can we recognise that we are dealing with an army? Next to the unit, which at the moment symbolises an army, a small shield appears, indicating the number of detachments included in the army. Clicking on an army we can see (at the bottom of the screen) 10 slots (windows), in which the detachments subordered to the army are located. We can get information about each of them by clicking the RMB on it.

##### Dividing units:

5. Building a fortress. A fortress can be built only at designated places (building sites). When reaching such a place, choose from the menu the icon “build a fortress” (No. 5). The detachments participating in the work disappear until the building is completed, and then appear in the hutments inside the fortress. The building work may be cancelled at any time (to get the working detachments back), but in this case we are losing all that has been created, and the site, on which no other fortress or castle can be built again. Note: A newly built castle must be given a name (a special window will appear); if you ignore this, the name will be given by the computer.

6. Building a castle - analogous to point 5, with the only difference that from the menu we choose the icon “build a castle” (No. 6).

#### Creating an ambush

An ambush can be created only by a detachment with at least silver status (see chapter Characteristics of Units/Attributes/Status). The cost of the command - 0 AP. For this purpose mark a detachment/an army and from the controlling menu choose the icon “ambush” (No. 6). The detachment, which creates an ambush, is invisible to the enemy’s detachments (except detachments with the gold status - these can see every ambush). Each enemy’s detachment, which crosses a spot occupied by a detachment waiting in ambush, is attacked. The ambush creates a panic among the attacked, thanks to which during the battle the player possesses two rounds of movement against each round of the enemy. At any time the player who created an ambush can remove it. If an enemy discovers the ambush, the hidden detachment becomes visible to all the enemy’s detachments, and if the detachment launches an attack, there is no advantage of a surprise effect.

*Note: You CANNOT create an ambush, if the detachment selected to it is within sight of an enemy’s unit (an appropriate notice would inform us about that).*

### CHAPTER 4.

### CHARACTERISTICS OF UNITS AND SPECIAL FIGURES.

While playing Clash you will find on the main map two types of figures: military units (symbolised by a single icon of the form of a soldier of a given type) and special figures (commanders).

#### 4.1. Units

There are 33 types of detachments in the game, identical for each player. Basically, they can be divided into two kinds:

1. conventional - by which we mean detachments armed with existing arms and in which ordinary people serve.
2. unearthly - in those creatures from the world of fantasy (Pegasus, elves, trolls, dragons, sorcerers, demons) and gigantic animals (scorpions, eagles, etc.).

Each detachment available on the main map has its information menu, which can be opened with the RMB. Detachments in the Clash move along the map, controlled by icons, which are located at the bottom right corner of the screen. They can be moved separately or in groups, called armies, consisting of at most 10 units. Each detachment can join every other detachment (creating an army) or join an existing army (unless the army already consists of 10 detachments). Two armies can also be merged, unless the total of detachments of both exceeds 10 units. Obviously, the process can be reversed at any time - one can disband an army, or select from it a number of units chosen by a player.

An army is moved with the speed equal to the number of AP’s possessed by the less mobile detachment. If the army contains unearthly detachments, which can fly, and detachments moving on the ground, the flying detachments still move by flying, but are restricted only to the locations available to the “ground” detachments (eg. they cannot fly over swamps). If an army contains at least one detachment with a status high enough (for example allowing to create an ambush), then the whole army is treated as possessing that status. During a battle an army is split into elements automatically, and after the battle is finished (the army who has won or has retreated) is automatically unified.

## Printed page 15

Source: [PDF page 9, left](Manual.pdf#page=9).

Each detachment has attacking or defending power and sometimes has also the ability to discharge distant fires. However, for some detachments their attacking or defending power is so small, that it is suggested to use them only for distant fires (like archers) or use them during a siege of a castle (battering ram for breaking through the walls).

#### 4.1.1. Detachment’s attributes

Any detachment, independently of the type, possesses permanent basic features and can have additional features, permanently or temporarily.

Basic features are: Power (to Attack or Defend), Action Points, Fire Power, Fire Range, Tiredness Degree, Manpower, Morale, Status.

1. Power is a unit used to figure out the result of a battle of detachments. The initial power can be increased (by training, type of terrain, type of attack) and decreased (by tiredness, type of terrain, reduced initial manpower).

2. Action Points is the number of points attached to a detachment in a round, which can be used for any possible action (movement, building work, etc.). AP’s are modified by the Tiredness Degree.

3. Fire Power is a unit used to estimate the damage in a detachment attacked from a distance. It is modified by the manpower of the detachment, its morale, tiredness degree.

4. Fire range is the distance at which a detachment with that ability can fire.

5. Manpower - is the manpower of a detachment, expressed in percentages. Initially, each detachment has 100% manpower. It influences the level of Power (to attack or defend) and the Fire Power. The manpower of a detachment can be made up in castles and fortresses.

6. Morale - or a “fighting spirit” of a detachment. The level of morale influences the power to attack or to defend. Low morale can lead to disbanding of a detachment or the detachment may ignore orders given to it until its morale increases. Initial morale of conventional detachments = 10 pt., for unearthly detachments = 6 pt. The highest morale level is 20 pt.

Morale increases:

- As a consequence of a won battle.
- As a consequence of training (i.e. achieving a higher status).
- Gradually achieves the initial level (if it was lower) if a unit stays in a castle.
- If the detachment is joined by a commander.

*Note: another commander, added to a detachment which already has got one, does not increase either the morale or any other parameters of the detachment.*

Morale decreases:

- As a consequence of a lost battle (retreat).
- For each round in which the detachment has the Tiredness Degree greater than or equal to 90%.
- If a person, which increases the detachments morale, leaves it.

*Note: if a detachment contains more than one such person, then this happens only when the last such person leaves the detachment (i.e. the detachment no longer contains a person increasing its morale).*

7. Status - denotes the training level of a detachment, which, in turn, modifies the Power to Attack or to Defend. The higher status of a detachment, the greater its fighting ability; (greater attacking or defending power, the detachments with this ability may fire at the enemy more than once in a round) high status ensures the detachments other abilities (described below).

Source: [PDF page 9, right](Manual.pdf#page=9).

There are 4 basic status levels:

- Initial (symbolised by a doublet in detachments characteristics).
- Medium (brown armour).
- High (silver armour).
- Elite (golden armour).

There are also so called “small points”, symbolised by small swords. Collecting three such swords moves us up to the next status level automatically.

High status (silver) allows creating of an ambush, and the elite status always makes the detachment able to notice an enemy’s ambush. The status increases by a point (a sword) while the detachment is trained. Detachments participating in a victorious battle increase their status by a level.

8. Additional features are: increased mobility (some detachments in some locations, such as highlanders in mountains), ability to create an ambush, ability to set traps (only builders), ability to fly, etc.

A special type are peasants. They can appear on the map in two forms:

1. Levy in mass - meaning a poor quality detachment, which can participate in a battle.
2. Wayfarers - peasants transferred from a castle. They differ from levy in mass peasants in that they carry bundles instead of arms. Wayfarers cannot participate in battles, cannot attack or defend themselves. If attacked by another player’s detachment, they pass under its command. The player can then direct the peasants to an owned castle, increasing this way possessed population of peasants.

#### 4.2. Special Figures

These include:

- A king (a player embodies it). The king does not appear in the game as a person (he is not seen on the main map).
- A queen - similarly as a king is not seen on the main map, but her actions will often influence the development of the game, and you will see her portrait on entering the building “court” in the castle’s yard. The queen will appear in the game after some time, which depends, among others, on your progress in the game.
- Commanders - these are the only individual persons that can be seen on the main map. One of a commander’s duties is increasing the fighting abilities of the commanded detachments.
- Sons and daughters of the king - royal children play the role of commanders in Clash.

The queen appears - you start the game as “marriageable king”. However, if reaching a proper status (plenty of gold, etc.) the marriage possibility appears - an envoy will appear with a proposal of a marriage with an overseas queen. A marriage has both advantages and disadvantages (you will find out during the game). Do your best to make the queen happy.

Queen’s whims - as a woman, she will cost you a lot of pieces of gold from time to time - if you, however, do not please her, it may cause unpleasant consequences. To indulge her whims you use all the gold which is located in all your buildings at that time (a proportional part of the whim’s cost will be taken from each of them).

Commanders - Commanders increase the fighting force of detachments under their command. If a commander is joined by a detachment (or the other way round) the Power of Attack, the Defence Power and the Striking Power (if possesed) and striking frequency of the army increases, as if the army had the highest status (golden one). After a commander leaves a detachment, the detachment (or detachments) returns to the previous values of its power of attack, defence, status etc.

A commander moves as an individual person along the game field. When a detachment joins him (or if he joins a detachment) he will occupy one of the army’s slots. During a battle a commander

The commander, whose detachment has been exterminated during a battle, is taken captive and becomes a prisoner. A prisoner is automatically taken to the closest castle of the victorious player. Prisoners - these are commanders, taken captive during battles. The prisoners live only for a short time, a few rounds (the dungeons are not very comfortable places to live in). During this time you can treat the prisoner in three ways:

### CHAPTER 5.

### BUILDINGS AND STRUCTURES IN THE CLASH GAME

#### 5.1. Buildings

The following buildings already exist when a game starts:

##### Temples

In this place one can pray. However, only the first detachment, which enters it, can make use of the chance given by the temple. Usually, the results would be positive (like reduction of tiredness, a detachment sent from heaven, an artefact enforcing some feature of a detachment), also sometimes negative (destruction of the detachment). Each part of the conflict (Heretics / Christians) has its own temples. Praying in the enemy’s temple would for sure result in a negative way (it pays to click on it before entering it). A temple cannot be destroyed.

##### Port

Ships from the Old Country arrive here, with mercenaries and adventurers, who - to be honest - do not care about which side they would fight on. They would readily join anyone who would offer them co-operation. Therefore, it pays to visit the port from time to time. If, in the meantime, mercenaries have arrived there, they would join your detachment right away.

And the following are the buildings that can be created by a player:

##### Castles

These are constructed by builders, in specially designated places only (see the paragraph “Structures” later in this chapter). The time creating a castle depends on the number of builders engaged to execute it, however, even with the maximum number of builders the matter is not an

## Printed page 18

Source: [PDF page 10, right](Manual.pdf#page=10).

**[Transcription note: Continuation of the sentence from printed page 17.]**

easy one (long waiting time). A castle is surrounded by walls, which can be developed. There are several advantages of possessing a castle: we can create new detachments there, train them and also complete the losses they suffered. The taxes from peasants are also collected there. Moreover, a court is located there, in which we can deal with family matters and keep prisoners. One can also find statistical data there, showing ones progress in the game compared with other players. Each player’s castle looks slightly different, both on the main map and in the “castle’s yard” mode. However, the location of all the buildings is the same in each castle.

At most 12 detachments of soldiers may stay in a castle at the same time (remember that, if you enter a castle in which 8 detachments are already staying, with an army of 6 detachments). Peasants live in a castle. We are warning you, that too large a number of peasants inhabiting a castle increases the possibility of a plague, which would decimate them mercilessly. One can assume that the population of 1000 peasants is the optimal one. It is worth transferring the excess peasants into those castles, where the deficit of labour force exists.

A castle may be overtaken by another player, and also destroyed by its owner (see the chapter Controlling).

The destruction of a castle results in the following:

- All the gold, detachments and special figures, belonging to the player, will automatically be transferred outside before the castle’s destruction.
- All the prisoners die.

A Castle can also be seized. If this happens:

- The gold, peasants and all the purchased licenses become the property of the conqueror.
- Prisoners - if some of your men are among them, they come back to your service. The other player’s commanders, if imprisoned there, become your prisoners.
- The special figures of the defeated player become your prisoners.

Buildings inside the castle / fortress:

- Hutments - the troops are stationed there. You get one after completing the building.
- Barracks - developed hutments. The production of soldiers and purchase of licences for new types of detachments can be done there; also commanders and detachments can stay there. The upgrade cost: 200 pieces of gold.
- School - a place in which to train detachments. The cost: 400 pieces of gold.
- Workshop - a place where it is possible to create distant striking detachments (archers, etc.) and war machines. The cost: 190 pieces of gold.
- Forge - a place to produce cavalry and heavy-armed detachments. The costs: 230 pieces of gold.
- Hospital - allows the completion of losses and reduces the possibility of plague in the castle, and in case of a plague it decreases it’s negative effects. The cost: 200 pieces of gold.
- Court - domestic affairs, in general. You get one after completing the building.

The increasing of the wall’s defensive potential is also possible (increase its resistance to enemy attacks). Two upgrades (developments) of walls are possible. Next upgrade of the walls is possible only after a higher level of technology is achieved (you will find more about this later in this manual). The upgrade is performed within one round and there is no cost for it. A damaged wall is repaired automatically (after a battle is over!), with no need for any action of a player, within a number of rounds which depends on the degree of damage. This does not cost the player any gold.

Creating buildings inside a castle is associated with technological development, so we advise you to build all that is possible.

You will find more details about creating buildings inside a castle in the chapter “Controlling”.

#### Fortress

This is a slightly reinforced castle (has stronger walls). No peasants can occupy a fortress, and this means, that a fortress does not produce any income. Only in fortresses the production of some kinds of detachments is possible, and training or curing is faster than in castles.

#### Watchtower

A small castle, with no military significance, but allows:

- To reduce the degree of tiredness of the detachments which have entered it.
- Enlarges the field of vision, i.e. uncovers the hidden sectors of the map in the watchtower’s neighbourhood.

#### Technology

As you develop your forces, you will have the opportunity to rise onto a higher technological level. This is associated with the possibility of producing better detachments, buying licenses for new units, developing castle’s walls, etc.

3 technological levels exist in Clash. You start on the first one. You reach the second level when you have two castles (or fortresses) in which all possible buildings have been built. The third level requires three such castles. If you reach a higher technological level, all your castles are automatically upgraded to the new level, and all the castles which are built after, start on the current technological level. There is no possibility of losing the level of technology you have reached. No upgrade of your castles results from the seizure of an enemy’s castle of a technological level higher than yours (unless you satisfy the requirements to rise onto a higher technological level because of the seizure). Obviously, you would be able to produce detachments there and to purchase licenses for detachments appropriate for it’s technology level. The level of technological development can be easily recognised by a glimpse at the castle’s information icon (on the main map or on the castle’s yard - the one with cog-wheels).

#### 5.2. Structures

By this notion we mean all those elements, which are not described in the section “Buildings”, and appear on the map (obviously, we do not mean trees or mountains!). The structures are also divided into those which already exist, and those created by players during the game.

Existing structures:

1. Foundations - in these places (and only there!) one can build new castles and fortresses.
2. Treasury fields - digging (by builders) in these places (often) provides valuable findings. Each of these fields can be used only once.
3. Country roads - allow movement with minimal AP cost.
4. River fords (shallows) - one can build a bridge across the river there. Shallows are the places, where sand under the water can be seen, and the water is brighter.

Structures made by a player/players:

1. Paved roads - have exactly the same properties as country roads, may be built (by builders) through every piece of land available for conventional detachments, and also on the existing country roads.
2. Bridges - allow crossing a river. Can be built only in selected parts of a river (shallows). Caution: wooden bridges deteriorate after some time and become useless - must be rebuilt (there is no possibility of conserving a deteriorating bridge).
3. Pitfalls - disguised pits in the ground with pointed stakes, are dangerous traps for careless enemies. A detachment (or an army) which is trapped there, suffers heavy casualties (even complete destruction is possible).

A battle starts when detachments of one player try to enter a spot occupied by a detachment or a building of another player. At this moment the place on which the battle has started is zoomed into.

A battle is fought on a special map, in the system of rounds. At the beginning of a round the detachments are placed automatically. The troops possess then the initial number of AP’s, which means that neither the current tiredness degree nor the currently possesed number of AP’s are taken into account (for example, if detachment X had initially 20 AP, and currently has 7 AP and 50% of tiredness, it starts the battle with the 20 AP). During the battle troops do not increase their tiredness by moving across the battle field. Only one unit can stay on a given spot at one time. One can not cross through ones own units while moving a detachment.

A battle can be fought in two modes:

1. Manual controlling - a player fully controls every action of his/her detachments during the battle.
2. Automatic - you are only informed about the results of the battle (you do not see it).

Before every battle a player can set the mode in which the battle will be fought. When you attack an enemy, a window appears on the main map, where you can choose the preferred mode of the battle. The mode 1 is suggested - unless you do not trust your generalship. The first round of movements is performed by the attacking player. During a battle the controlling of detachments is executed via icons, which can be found in the screen’s corner. The icons are:

Legend

1. shooting
2. ride
3. prepare to defence
4. next detachment
5. retreat (escape from the battle field)

---

1. Shooting - concerns the detachments which can fire at a distance. The cursor changes into an arrow. We move it onto a detachment (or onto castles’ walls) and press LMB. Note: the number of shots allowed in a round is shown in the information panel, in the “fire power” window. The number of arrows there shows how many times a given detachment can fire in one round. Detachments with little experience can only fire once during a round, even if possessing a sufficient amount of AP. The firing

cost is 5 AP for a volley. You have to remember, that each weapon has its limited range, so not always firing at enemy’s detachments is possible, especially if they are at a long distance from attacking forces. Also, firing at an enemy, who is located on a neighbouring spot (which is next to the spot occupied by the firing detachment) with guns or catapults, is not possible.

2. Ride (battle rage) - after ordering that command (cost 0 AP) the detachment flies into a battle rage and inflicts about 25% more damage than in a standard attack. However, its losses are much heavier. We dissuade a charge on a stronger enemy!

3. Defence - to make use of that option you must have minimum 5 AP, but the order causes that all the AP’s, which the given detachment has, are used in that round. Defence increases defensive power of the detachment. A detachment, which was given this order, remains under its influence as long as either it stays in the same place or does not grapple (can keep firing). Moving the detachment or ordering it to attack/a charge, will cancel the results of the defence order. The detachment in this mode is marked (on the map) with a shield, which is not visible to the enemy during his/her round of movement.

4. Next detachment - our next detachment will be marked on the battle map.

5. Retreat - ends the battle. The detachments of the player who ordered the retreat, escape in panic from the battle field, losing extra (which is symbolised by the victors pursuit of the escaping). In addition to that, the retreat causes the decrease of their morale.

Above these icons we see (the picture at the beginning of the chapter) the information panel of the currently marked unit (power of attack, defending power, morale, number of AP’s, etc.).

To limit the passive behaviour of players during the battle, “the wrath of god” option was introduced. it means that the player, who does not make any move with his/her detachment in two rounds, exposes himself to... a bolt from the blue, which decreases by 25% the initial effective force of each of his/her detachments. It pays to encircle the enemy’s detachments during the battle (this means that on the spots next to the enemy’s unit there are at least three of our units). The encirclement is marked on the screen with special arrows. The more the neighbouring spots occupied by our detachments, the weaker its defence (suffers heavier losses). It happens sometimes, that players... encircle each other. Never mind, each of them, in his round of attack, will inflict more damage on the other player.

The battle ends when:

1. One of the players has lost all the detachments on the map.
2. One of the players has ordered a retreat.

The effects of the battle:

Although the morale and status of victorious detachments increases, one has to remember that commanders fight in some detachments (though not present personally on the map during the battle). In case of a player, who has lost all his/her units - the commander is captured and sent to the nearest castle of the victor, and becomes a prisoner (appears there in the next round of that player). If, at that moment, the victorious player has no castle, the prisoner dies. In case of retreat, the commander retreats with his detachments, which suffer extra (quite heavy) losses, their morale decreases, etc.

A variant of a battle is a siege of a castle.

The difference is in that the defenders are hidden behind the walls, which are an obstacle for all the conventional detachments of the enemy, fighting at close quarters. The flying detachments fly over the walls without problem, and the distant fire detachments can fire at the enemy who is inside the walls (and at the walls as well). The walls consist of a series of segments, each of which can be

## Printed page 22

Source: [PDF page 12, right](Manual.pdf#page=12).

**[Transcription note: Continuation of the siege description from printed page 21.]**

destroyed (particularly efficient in the destruction are guns, catapults and battering rams). The destroyed segment of a wall is no longer an obstacle for conventional detachments. The defenders can lead their conventional troops inside and outside the castle/fortress through the gate. From the enemy’s perspective, the gate is a segment of a wall (which cannot be crossed until it is destroyed). If necessary, the defenders can destroy the walls of their castle themselves. During the siege of a castle the defending player cannot order a retreat - he/she must fight to the end. The castle with no defending detachment inside, surrenders without fighting, if attacked. The walls damaged during a battle are repaired automatically (after the battle) within 1 or more rounds (depending on the degree of damage).

A special kind of battle is an ambush.

During the battle, the party which created the ambush has - because of the surprise of the enemy - the right to two rounds of movements, while the enemy has to content itself with one round only.
